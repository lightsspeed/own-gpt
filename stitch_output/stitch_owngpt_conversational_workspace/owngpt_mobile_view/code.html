Can you explain the difference between quantum entanglement and superposition in simple terms?

Think of it this way:

Superposition is like a spinning coin. Before it lands, it's technically both heads and tails at the same time. In quantum physics, a particle exists in all possible states simultaneously until we measure it.

Entanglement is like having two "magic" coins. If you flip one and it lands on heads, the other coin instantly lands on tails, no matter how far apart they are. They are perfectly linked across space.
